Overview

A four-bar linkage built from a bearing, crank, extension, and rocker, with a second bearing fixed 130 mm away. The mechanism was assembled with hinge joints, then run through a SolidWorks Motion analysis driven by a single rotary motor at 20 rpm. The study covers two full cycles and tracks motion paths, velocities, motor torque, and bearing reaction forces. The motion traces were exported to CSV and layered in Excel for motor analysis.

FIG. 2. Motor torque (top left), crank angular displacement (bottom left), and reaction force (right) over two cycles.

What I did

  • Assembled the four-bar mechanism in SolidWorks from the Bearing, Crank, Extension, and Rocker parts, with two fixed bearings 130 mm apart and hinge joints between the links.
  • Ran the motion solver to find redundant joints, then resolved them by fixing the bearings and changing joint types until the mechanism had exactly one degree of freedom.
  • Added a rotary motor to the crank at 20 rpm and ran a 6 second study, giving two full cycles at 3 seconds each.
  • Traced the motion paths of points at one quarter, one half, and three quarters of the Extension's length.
  • Traced the X and Y velocities of the Extension's midpoint, exported the traces to CSV, and layered them in Excel for motor analysis.
  • Changed every part to plain carbon steel, added gravity to the study, and plotted motor torque and bearing reaction forces.

Negative motor torque

A negative motor torque means the motor is applying torque opposite to the direction of rotation. Instead of driving the crank against gravity as expected, the motor is working to slow the rotation down.
